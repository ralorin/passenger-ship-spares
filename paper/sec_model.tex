\subsection{The Decision on Board}
\label{sec:decision}
For one class of power-electronic unit a ship carries $P$ references with
$N_p$ installed units each, known from its equipment register. The
replaceable unit is the driver, supply or drive module the crew exchanges. A
\emph{reference} is a group of units with a common spare: units belong to
different references when rating, dimming interface, mounting or class
approval differ, and units with a common approved replacement are merged. A
reference may be a functional family made by two makers; the \emph{exact
part} is the reference--maker pair, one per ship, since a class is fitted from
one maker. Ships share the reference effect, those of one maker also the maker
effect, and the ship--reference effect below belongs to one exact part on one
ship. The \emph{horizon} is the interval until the next effective
replenishment opportunity, a port call at which ordered spares can be loaded.
It equals a cruise only for a ship resupplied at every turnaround, as the
fleet of Section~\ref{sec:fleet} assumes; otherwise it spans several cruises
(Section~\ref{sec:ship}).

A \emph{failure} is the loss of function of an installed unit, a
\emph{replacement need} a failure that must be made good from stock before
resupply, and a \emph{store issue} a spare drawn. A repair deferred to port
removes a failure from the need; an approved equivalent from another
reference, or a unit repaired on board, moves or removes an issue; and when
stock runs out a need waits and is never issued, so issues understate need in
the missions that matter. With $Y_p$ the needs of reference $p$ over the
horizon, taken here and in every simulation as its failures (each failure
needs a spare before resupply), and $\mathbf s=(s_1,\dots,s_P)$ the
stocks, two criteria are distinguished:
\begin{align}
J(\mathbf s)&=\Pr\big(Y_p\le s_p\ \text{for all } p\big)\ \ge\ \gamma,
\label{eq:joint}\\
A(S)&=\Pr\Big(\textstyle\sum_p Y_p\le S\Big)\ \ge\ \gamma .
\label{eq:agg}
\end{align}
The joint criterion~\eqref{eq:joint} is the one a ship needs; the
aggregate criterion~\eqref{eq:agg} applies only within a merged group. With
unit costs $\mathbf c>0$ the decision is
\begin{equation}
\min_{\mathbf s\in\mathbb N^P}\ \mathbf c^\top\mathbf s
\quad\text{subject to}\quad J(\mathbf s)\ge\gamma .
\label{eq:ip}
\end{equation}
Only quantities known before sailing enter the prediction: product, maker,
age, zone, planned itinerary and its climate normals, booked occupancy and the
planned powered hours that follow; realised conditions enter the evaluation.

\subsection{Records and Model}
\label{sec:mechanisms}
A \emph{cell} is the units of one reference, on one ship, in one zone and band
of age, over one mission, with $y_c$ failures over $H_c$ powered unit-hours.
Populations with no failure stay in the fit; preventive replacements are
excluded from $y_c$ but keep their hours; a common-cause failure is logged as
one incident. The count of cell $c$ is modelled as
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
y_c\mid\mathbf b\sim\mathrm{NB}(\mu_c,\phi),\\
\log\mu_c=\log H_c+\mathbf x_c^{\top}\boldsymbol\beta+\textstyle\sum_k b^{(k)}_{\ell_k(c)},
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
with $b^{(k)}_\ell\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_k^2)$ independent over groupings $k$
and levels $\ell$. The negative binomial is a Poisson count with an
independent $\mathrm{Gamma}(\phi,\phi)$ multiplier per cell, so $\phi$ acts at
the scale of the cell: a reference total sums cells with their own
multipliers, and predictions use the cell partition of the fit, so a horizon
of several missions has one multiplier per mission cell while the latent
effects persist. The ship part
of the latent log rate of reference $p$ on ship $s$ is
\begin{equation}
u_s+w_{sp},\qquad u_s\sim\mathcal N(0,\tau^2),\quad w_{sp}\sim\mathcal N(0,\omega^2),
\label{eq:structure}
\end{equation}
where $\tau$ is \emph{ship-wide} heterogeneity, shared by every reference on a
ship (electrical environment, supply quality, maintenance), and $\omega$ the
\emph{ship--reference interaction} (an installation batch, a zone-specific
duty). Exact parts are nested in manufacturer and crossed with ship.

\subsection{Estimation}
Let $\boldsymbol\psi=(\boldsymbol\beta,\mathbf b)$ have dimension $d$ and let
$\boldsymbol\theta=(\log\sigma_k,\log\phi)$. The fit maximises over
$\boldsymbol\theta$ the Laplace approximation to
$\log\int p(\mathbf y\mid\boldsymbol\psi,\phi)\,p(\mathbf b\mid\boldsymbol\theta)
\,\pi(\boldsymbol\beta)\,\mathrm d\boldsymbol\psi$,
\begin{multline}
\ell_{\mathrm{LA}}(\boldsymbol\theta)=\log p(\mathbf y\mid\hat{\boldsymbol\psi},\phi)
+\log\pi(\hat{\boldsymbol\beta})\\
-\sum_k\Big[\frac{q_k}{2}\log(2\pi\sigma_k^2)+\frac{\|\hat{\mathbf b}^{(k)}\|^2}{2\sigma_k^2}\Big]\\
+\frac d2\log 2\pi-\frac12\log\det\mathbf H_{\boldsymbol\theta},
\label{eq:objective}
\end{multline}
where $q_k$ is the number of levels of grouping $k$,
$\hat{\boldsymbol\psi}(\boldsymbol\theta)$ the joint mode and
$\mathbf H_{\boldsymbol\theta}$ the negative Hessian there~\cite{tierney1986}.
The prior $\pi$ is flat for the intercept and $\mathcal N(0,5^2)$ for the
other coefficients. Since $\boldsymbol\beta$ is integrated with $\mathbf b$,
\eqref{eq:objective} is of restricted type~\cite{breslow1993}, and maximising
it is empirical-Bayes estimation; its error grows when levels carry few
failures, so tests, intervals and plans are checked by simulation. The mode is
found by damped Newton steps and $\boldsymbol\theta$ by L-BFGS-B, with standard
deviations capped at 3 in the fleet and street-lighting fits.

A variance component is tested by the likelihood ratio of~\eqref{eq:objective}
with and without it, referred to the equal mixture of $\chi^2_0$ and
$\chi^2_1$~\cite{self1987}, whose 5\,\% point is 2.71. That limit is for the
exact likelihood with one component on the boundary and the others interior;
here the criterion is approximate, samples are finite~\cite{crainiceanu2004},
and when the other component is also near zero the limit has more terms, so
the size is measured in Section~\ref{sec:design}, including splits with both
components zero. Profile intervals invert the same criterion and, to agree with
the test, contain zero when the statistic is below 2.71.

\subsection{Predictive Distribution}
For a population not used in fitting, such as the next mission of a ship, the
decision needs the predictive law of its counts, integrating over
$\boldsymbol\psi$, the effects $\mathbf b^\ast$ of levels absent from the fit,
and $\boldsymbol\theta$. The \emph{predictive} plan fixes
$\boldsymbol\theta=\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}$ and draws $\boldsymbol\psi$ from
$\mathcal N(\hat{\boldsymbol\psi},\hat{\mathbf H}^{-1})$, with
$\hat{\mathbf H}=\mathbf H_{\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}}$;
it omits the uncertainty of the variance components. The \emph{bootstrap}
plan propagates it approximately: $B$ data sets are simulated from the fitted
model on the observed cells and exposures; $\boldsymbol\theta$ is re-estimated
on each; for each $\boldsymbol\theta_b$, $\boldsymbol\psi$ is drawn from the
Laplace approximation \emph{on the observed data}, followed by unseen effects,
gamma multipliers and Poisson counts; and the draws are pooled. The
re-estimates follow the sampling distribution of the estimator, not the
posterior, so Section~\ref{sec:design} checks how far they serve in its place.
In both plans a vector is chosen from the draws and evaluated on data not
used to choose it.
